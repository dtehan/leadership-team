Teradata account team tool

Leadership Team Twin

Rehearse the conversation with your customer's executives before you have it.

What it is

A simulated leadership team for one customer account: their CEO, CFO, CTO and COO, plus a Moonshot thinker and a Contrarian. You give it a decision or a pitch idea. They debate it over several rounds, the CEO twin decides, and you get a one-page decision memo and the full discussion to read.

It runs in Claude Desktop as a skill, in a normal chat. Everything it knows about the customer comes from public information (10-Ks, earnings, interviews, press) and anything you add.

It is a simulation, not a prediction. The twins are built from public and supplied information. They are not what the real executives said or will do. Use the output to sharpen your thinking, never to quote a customer or to put words in their mouths.

Why it matters to you

Account executives rarely get to test an idea against the customer's executive team. This is the next best thing.

Pressure-test before you pitch

See the objections the CFO, CTO and COO are most likely to raise, and the one you did not see coming, before you are in the room.

Speak each executive's language

Learn how each role would weigh the same idea: ROI and risk for the CFO, architecture for the CTO, execution and disruption for the COO.

Pick the right angle and sponsor

Find out which executive is likely to champion the idea, which one blocks it, and what would change their vote.

Prepare in minutes, not days

Account research, executive profiles and a first-draft objection plan, without building them by hand.

1Install the skill (about 2 minutes, once)

You need Claude Desktop, signed in. Nothing else to install.

You were sent three skill zips. Each does one job:

ZipWhat it does
account-brief-builderBuilds the customer's account brief from public research and your own knowledge.
leadership-team-twinRuns the debate and writes the decision memo. Also records what the customer really decided.
guest-specialist-builderDefines a guest expert (security, analytics...) for a customer.
  1. Save the three zips. Do not unzip them.
  2. In Claude Desktop open Settings → Capabilities → Skills and choose Upload skill. Pick a zip. Repeat for each.
  3. Switch all three on.
Don't see Skills? Skills need "code execution and file creation" switched on in Capabilities. On a Team or Enterprise plan your Claude admin may have to allow skills first. Ask them, and mention you are using the Teradata account-team skill.

2Create one Project per customer (about 1 minute each)

A Claude Project is the sealed box for one customer. It holds that customer's background, and keeps one customer's information out of another's.

  1. Click Projects → New project and name it for the customer, for example Acme Corp: leadership team.
  2. In the project's instructions, paste: 
    This project is for Acme Corp only. Use the leadership-team-twin skill.
Never use facts about any other customer.
  3. Start every chat for that customer inside this project.

The most important habit: save it, then load it back

Claude does not remember one chat in the next. It only knows what is in the project. Everything the skills create (the brief, the memos, the guest cards) is a file that you save to your computer and then add back to the project. Do this every time.

The routine: 5 steps, every time

  1. Ask Claude for the file. If Claude shows the result only in the chat, reply "Give me that as a downloadable file."
  2. Download it. Click the file's download / save button under Claude's reply.
  3. Save it to your Desktop, in a folder per customer, for example Desktop / Leadership Team / Acme / . Keep the date in the file name. This folder is your copy and your backup.
  4. Load it into the project. Open the customer's project, find Project knowledge (the files panel), click + / Add content → Upload from device, and choose the file from your Desktop folder.
  5. Delete the old version from the project (briefs only; keep every memo, outcome note and guest card). Then start a new chat in the project, because an open chat won't see the new file.
Check it worked: in a new chat in the project, ask "What files do you have for this customer?" The brief, memos, outcome notes and guest cards should be listed.

3Build the account brief (about 10 minutes, once)

In a chat inside the project, type:

You saySet up Acme Corp and build the account brief.
(uses account-brief-builder)

Claude researches the company's financials, technology, strategy and named executives from public sources (turn on web search if you have it, or attach the annual report). It then asks a few things only you know: relationships, politics, what you have heard in meetings, incumbent vendors. Your answers make the twins much sharper. Check the CEO, CFO, CTO and COO names and titles are right.

Now save and load it back (routine above): download the brief, save it to Desktop / Leadership Team / Acme /, and upload it to the project's knowledge. Until you do, the next chat will not know the customer.

Keep it current

Before an important meeting, or every few weeks, open a new chat in the project and type:

You sayRefresh the Acme Corp brief. What's changed?
(uses account-brief-builder)

Claude reads the brief you saved, finds what's new since its date (earnings, leadership changes, announcements, technology news), asks what has changed on your side, and gives you an updated brief with a change log. Your own account notes are never overwritten by public news. Download the new file, save it next to the old one on your Desktop, upload it to the project, and delete the old brief from the project. Then use a new chat.

4Ask the team a question

Plain English is fine. Good questions are decisions or pitches, not trivia.

You sayConvene the team on: should we pitch consolidating their three data platforms into one analytics foundation this quarter, led by the CIO?

The debate plays out in the chat: opening reactions, several rounds of argument, a vote, the CEO's decision, a one-page decision memo and what to do next as the account executive.

Now save and load it back: ask for the memo as a file, save it to your Desktop folder, and upload it to the project. Future questions will read it, so the team remembers earlier decisions.

A worked example

Illustrative only. "Northwind Retail" is a made-up company, and these are not real executives or real output. A real run is longer and uses the customer's actual facts.

You, the Teradata account executive
Convene the team on: should we pitch a single governed data foundation to consolidate their three analytics platforms before peak season?
CFO twin
What does it cost, and when does it pay back? Show me the run-rate savings from retiring two platforms and the migration spend. If the payback is longer than two budget cycles I'm a no.
COO twin
Not before peak season. A migration that disrupts forecasting in Q4 is a risk I can't carry. Sequence it for January.
CTO twin
Three platforms means three copies of the truth and slow AI projects. I like consolidation if it keeps our existing tools working and doesn't force a rewrite.
Contrarian twin
Who owns this? If it's pitched as an IT project it dies in budget review. What business outcome does the CEO care about?
CEO twin: decision
Proceed, but not as a Q4 migration. Start with a phased proof on one use case, tied to a business outcome, with a January cutover plan and a clear payback model.

What the account executive does with this

  • Reframes the pitch from "platform consolidation" to a phased proof tied to a business outcome.
  • Builds the payback model before the CFO asks for it.
  • Avoids proposing a Q4 migration. That is the COO's red line.
  • Makes the CTO the technical champion and finds the business sponsor the Contrarian says is missing.

Ground rules

  • One customer, one project, one chat. For a different customer, open that customer's project. The skill will refuse to mix two customers in one chat.
  • Public information plus your own notes. Don't paste confidential customer or Teradata material you aren't cleared to use with Claude. Follow Teradata's AI-use policy.
  • Public, professional facts only about real people.
  • Treat output as a rehearsal. Never quote it as what the customer said or will do.
  • It only remembers what you save and load back. Claude chats don't carry over. Download every brief, memo, outcome note and guest card, keep it in your Desktop folder, and upload it to the project.
